## 4. The open decision that everything else waits on

Sean asked for a rigorous, non-agreeable evaluation of a proposed evolution of the
RepID scoring model; Grok then proposed a Bayesian (Beta-Binomial /
Dirichlet-Multinomial) formulation. The synthesis below is the current position.
**It is the single most important thing in this file.**

### What the Bayesian move buys, and it is more than it first looks

**It may dissolve L3 rather than answer it.** L3 is a *delta-accumulation*
pathology — the score is a running sum and something clamps the sum, which is
exactly why the ledger and the state could disagree (L9). In a posterior
formulation there is no delta to absorb: a fault updates `β`, and the score is
`f(posterior)` — computed, not accumulated. **The floor then becomes a *display*
decision (what tier a counterparty is shown) rather than a *state* decision (what
the score is).** That split is precisely the tension L3 was a values call about;
career protection is a display concern and free defection is a state concern, and
they only conflict today because they are the same number.

*Caveat, not decorative:* this only works if the floor moves to display. Keep a
floor on the posterior itself — clamp `β`, or floor the derived score before
storing — and the identical bug returns in Bayesian clothing, and
`floor-absorption-audit.ts` will not catch it because it looks for a different
shape.

**Uncertainty comes free, and that is what makes domain scoping safe.** With 16
domains at n=1, a point estimate has no business gating anything. A wide credible
interval fails a high-stakes gate on its own terms — no arbitrary volume threshold
to argue about. **This is worth building independently of whether the headline
score goes Bayesian:** compute a posterior purely for gating while the current
engine keeps producing the score.

**The parameter-count argument is real and my earlier sketch loses it.** My
value-weighted fault-density proposal had four hand-tuned knobs (γ, k, δ, `g_min`),
none identifiable from data. A Beta formulation has two (prior, decay), both with a
principled interpretation and both estimable from the ledger that already exists.
That is not close.

### The crux neither analysis stated cleanly: counts versus value

**Beta-Binomial is count-based.** One success is one pseudo-count whether it was a
trivial ping or a large settlement. That is exactly the dilution attack: farm many
trivial successes, defect once on something that matters, and the posterior mean
barely moves.

The repair is value-weighted evidence — increment by `wᵢ`, not by 1 (a weighted-
likelihood / power-prior construction, standard, not a hack). **But it breaks the
conjugate interpretation in one specific way that must be said out loud:** after
weighting, posterior *variance* no longer means "uncertainty from n observations."
It means "uncertainty from W units of value observed" — so one large interaction
produces a tight-looking posterior on almost no behavioural evidence.

> **Therefore: two statistics, not one.**
> **Value-weighted α/β drives the mean.** (Resists dilution.)
> **Raw n drives the confidence gate.** (Resists confidence inflation.)
> Collapse them into a single Beta and the distribution lies in one direction or
> the other. Keeping them apart costs one column.

**Decay carries the same asymmetry problem.** Decaying α and β together preserves
the mean and inflates the variance — correct in isolation, since old evidence
*should* widen the interval. But a dormant agent then slides back toward the prior,
so **dormancy becomes a partial reputation launder**: the L3 ratchet in a new dress,
*wait it out* instead of *floor it out*. Bad evidence must decay slower than good,
and that asymmetry is a **policy parameter that must be explicit and versioned**,
never implicit in a half-life.

### What does not change

**"No contest" is still the elected-scope proposal, and the objection stands.**
The confession discount works because **it prices an act** — self-reporting before
detection is verifiable and timestamped, which is why L7 was findable at all.
"No contest" prices a **plea**. The moment the pleader chooses the scope, the
dominant strategy is to plead in the domain where they have least at stake.
Bounding the magnitude caps the payoff of an always-weakly-dominant move; it does
not remove the dominance.

*The acceptable version:* if "no contest" means **"I waive dispute; the penalty
applies to the domain the evidence establishes, not the one I nominate"** — it is a
dispute-waiver, its only effect is saving arbitration cost, and it is fine. The line
is **who picks the scope**, not how big the discount is.

**The hierarchy is right eventually and is not today's binding constraint.** Over
the measured distribution a tree yields one fat node and sixteen leaves of size one.
Aggregation does not manufacture evidence. The fix for sparse domains is the
confidence gate; the gate is also what makes the tree *safe* to add, because a
parent rollup without one launders a sparse child's fault into a fat parent's
volume.

**Selective disclosure: agreed, low regret, one trap.** A domain-scoped proof needs
a **new statement (A2)** with a distinct arity or an explicit domain-separation tag
in the transcript — **not a field added to A1**, which would be silently unverified
(see §3). Five-minute decision now, unrecoverable later.

### New gaming surfaces this opens

1. **Dilution** via unweighted counts — above.
2. **Dormancy laundering** via symmetric decay — above.
3. **Scope shopping** via pleader-chosen "no contest" — above.
4. **Rollup laundering** — a hierarchy aggregating *counts* dilutes a sparse child
   into a fat parent. Aggregate **posteriors pessimistically** (worst-of-relevant)
   for gating; never average.
5. **The prior is an unaudited policy lever with enormous reach.** What is
   `α₀, β₀` for a brand-new agent in a brand-new domain? Optimistic → Sybil is
   cheap, because a fresh identity starts near where a good agent sits. Pessimistic
   → onboarding is impossible. **This is the new `floor_override`:** one number,
   huge behavioural consequence, no obvious owner. It must be inside
   `policy_version` from day one.

### An honest limitation to record rather than paper over

Beta-Binomial assumes **exchangeability**. A reputation system's entire purpose is
that agents change strategy in response to it — draws are not from a fixed urn. The
model degrades gracefully and nothing better is available, but credible intervals
will be **over-confident on exactly the adversarially adaptive agents that matter
most.** Use the interval for **gating**, where widening is the conservative
direction. **Never publish it as a calibrated probability.**

### Implementation trap that will bite immediately

`policy-version.ts` derives its digest by probing `deltaFor` and `assessRisk`. A
posterior engine's behaviour is determined by the **prior, the decay rates, and the
weighting function** — none of which are `deltaFor`. Ship posterior scoring without
extending the transcript and `pol1-37620edf769590dd` stays **byte-identical across a
total change of regime.** That is the exact hole XC found in the DB half,
reintroduced through the app half. **The transcript extension goes in the same
commit, and `policy-scope-check.ts` gets re-pinned alongside it.**

Minor but real: with a real-valued score derived from a posterior, the
half-away-from-zero rounding becomes load-bearing at tier boundaries in a way it is
not today.

## 5. Schema to lock now (revised)

| Lock | Why |
|---|---|
| `evidence_weight` (numeric) on the event | The `wᵢ`. **Separate from severity.** Without it no other weighting scheme can ever be re-run against history. |
| `run_state_at_event` → **pre- and post- sufficient statistics** (α, β or Dirichlet counts) plus the weight applied | **A posterior cannot be reconstructed from a ledger of point estimates.** Highest value on this list and the cheapest. |
| Raw `n` alongside weighted counts | The two-statistics split. One column. |
| Prior parameters recorded per event | The prior is a policy lever; it must be reconstructible per row. |
| `domain_id` separate from human label | Labels get renamed; identity must not. |
| `impact_mode` | Lets absolute and ratio impact coexist during shadow comparison. |
| Severity **inputs**, not only the severity output | You cannot re-score history from an output. |
