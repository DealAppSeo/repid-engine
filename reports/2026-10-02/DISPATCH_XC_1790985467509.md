**2. The sanitizer interaction**  
The pre-auth SQL-keyword sanitizer (`src/index.ts:289-361`) runs on **every** POST that is not explicitly exempted. The new `/api/v1/classify` route is not in the current bypass list (laya-classify is mounted but its path is `/laya/classify`, not exempted by name). **3. Cost and abuse**  
With no key, no paid model, no DB insert, and no HAL/quorum: the only cost is CPU cycles on the classification logic itself (cheap local heuristic or tiny local model).  

Cheapest DoS: Flood with minimal valid JSON payloads that pass the sanitizer (e.g. short text without `; -- SELECT` etc.) or that hit the 400 path. A per-IP limit (via `globalRateLimit` or `ipRateLimit` middleware) **does stop simple floods from one origin**. However, `app.set('trust proxy', 1)` + Railway edge means `req.ip` (and `x-forwarded-for` parsing in `ip-rate-limit.ts:clientIp`) is the **client IP** (first entry in X-Forwarded-For), not the proxy itself — so it works behind proxies/CDNs. A distributed botnet (many IPs) or proxy rotation would still succeed. Rate limit on 400 path is especially important to avoid amplifying CPU on rejected requests. **VERIFIED** (read `src/index.ts:169`, `src/middleware/ip-rate-limit.ts:1-290`, `src/middleware/rate-limit.ts`, and `clientIp` implementation).
